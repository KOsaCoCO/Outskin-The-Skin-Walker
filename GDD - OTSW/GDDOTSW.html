Overall game story, a rough idea on the progression.

—---------------

Skin-Walker is in the skin of a woman – Mari.

Mari is a woman who worked as a show master, transforming people to become the better version of themselves.

In this story, the Player comes onto the show, wanting to participate in a life changing story. Mari, is the upbeat, but also formal show-woman, who is their guide.

To participate, the Player must accept a contract that in case of an accident, lifts ‘The New Tomorrow’ studio, from any responsibility be it financial or mental.

Over the course of the story, Player converses with Mari as they both head off into the minigames, but there is a plot twist. The show is actually filled not with people, but with monsters!

It becomes a survival show of Mari VS the Player.

—-----------------

Details:

Gradually, over the course of minigames, either Mari or Player, lose sanity. From which the plot-twist of the show audience being monsters emerges. Either the Player’s sanity gets low and they finally witness the live audience as clapping, hooting monsters. Or Mari, loses its sanity, de-transforming into their real form – The SkinWalker

The player is a human, they come onto the show to become a better version of themselves.

But ends up having to fight against the witch Skin-Walker.

The player is able to try and deconstruct Mari’s image, either verbally knocking off the illusions (skin walkers possess mind control). Or winning in the minigames, earning the special bullets used to shoot a skin-walker :(salt water or holy water , can also shoot the witch with bullets dipped in white ash, but the shot must hit them in the neck or the hand, or a powerful shaman to force skin-waker turn in on itself)

Here are a few defining characteristics and abilities of skinwalkers:

  • Shapeshifting: They can transform into various animals, such as wolves or coyotes. They'll change forms depending on their needs. For example, if they need immense strength, they might become a bear. Skinwalkers assume these roles voluntarily — it's not a curse, like being a werewolf.
  • Supernatural speed: They move at incredibly fast speeds.
  • Mimicry: They can imitate human voices or animal sounds to deceive others.
  • Mind control: They influence or control the thoughts of others.
  • Cursing: They can cast harmful spells or curses.
  • Physical strength: They have superhuman strength.
  • Glowing eyes: According to legend, you can tell if you're in the presence of a skinwalker by their eyes. If you shine a light on one when they're in animal form, their eyes glow bright red. In human form, their eyes seem animal-like.

Who is Mari?

Why did skin-Walker become one?

Skin walkers are supposedly witches, who knew healing and charms and spells, but turned into malevolent monsters after killing a family member as a part of a ritual.

Mari, being a restrictive persona, who held up strict beliefs, always held herself closed. Yet began noticing the difference in how people treated her and her sister. Rather than assigning one emotion to each color, professional artists consider how multiple colors work together. A predominantly blue character with vibrant orange accents communicates something entirely different from a character dressed in only blue.

One of the simplest yet most effective techniques in color theory in character design is the 60–30–10 rule. Originally popularized in interior and graphic design, this principle has become a valuable framework for building balanced, memorable character palettes.

Rather than using multiple colors with equal importance, the rule establishes a clear visual hierarchy.

  • 60% Dominant Color – Defines the character’s identity and occupies most of the visual space.
  • 30% Secondary Color – Supports the dominant color while adding contrast and depth.
  • 10% Accent Color – Draws attention to important details such as eyes, accessories, logos, magical effects, or weapons.

This ratio creates harmony without making the design feel predictable.

Realistic villain - Frolo : https://youtu.be/WQFrThLnl2c

        Notes - >

1: Realism

2: Rigid righteousness

3: Disturbing Inner conflict

Strong conviction - intense beliefs that justify their actions as morally right, no matter how evil they are

Frollo believes to be the agent of divine power, he has the power above regular people, and his purpose is to rid France of the heathens.

In the opening Frollo murders the mother of the Hunchback, and is about to throw the child into a well, but is stopped, told it’s a sin. And that’s the only reason why he doesn’t kill the child. But it’s hypocritical, because he killed his mother.

That’s the part about his morality in believing he is wholly holy and righteous in his actions, and anything that isn't holy, he wouldn’t do.

His inner conflict is about lust for Esmeralda, and blames Esmeralda for making him feel like a sinner. Which then, would denote the belief that he isn’t holy. And he cannot allow that. He pursuits his obsession over Esmeralda, while trying to keep his status as saint, and control over people.

He begs for salvation, but blames Esmeralda for his sins.

Esmeralda helps the hunchback, saving the so-called ‘inferior’, in this case inferior is portrayal of purity, which conflicts with Frollo’s self-righteous beliefs. WHere he believes he is doing the good by killing people, and tries to keep the Hunchback in his control, isolating him and mocking him.  

Show their hypocrisy:

 Villains must be blind to their own flaws while condemning the others.

Frollo's inability to accept his sin, while punishing others for theirs, adds character depth, and makes fall from grace inevitable.

 

        

Make them relatable:

Mirrors real world issues

Frollo uses his religion and political power to justify prosecution and control, mirroring historical and modern abuse of authority.

Prejudices, lust, moral superiority, traits often in corrupt leaders - sense of realism.

If a villain lacks personal or social context, it doesn’t resonate well with real world issues.

Give them power:

Often hold authority, misuse for ideology game,

Frollo is minister of justice, enforcing his draconian beliefs to oops Romani people and oppress quasimodo.

Power is then not just symbolic, it allows action without consequences. - a more dangerous antagonist.

Show their downfall:

Downfall as a result to acknowledge their flaws.

Obsession with Esmeralda - his failure to see evil within himself, leads to his death.

His arc becomes satisfying and tragic.

Avoid exaggeration:

Ground in reality than supernatural elements

Frolo's evilness is impactful, because it's realistic. His Actions driven by his misuse of political and religious power, this has been seen throughout history this makes character more believable.

Use rationalisation:

This villain should constantly justify their actions as being for the greater good.

Frolo believes that every cruel act, burning homes, killing Romani people or persecuting Esmeralda, is justified because he is protecting his society from sin.

Highlights his twisted logic that drives his actions.

Give opportunities for redemption:

Is given the ability to change, but consistently chooses the wrong path.

Such as raising quasimodo with love or showing mercy to esmeralda. But he continues to choose cruelty and self-righteousness.

Contrast them with virtuous characters:

 Must have contrast to reveal villains' moral failings. Frolo is contrasted with Quasimodo, whose physical deformity belies his inner goodness and Esmeralda who is compassionate and brave . Their purity and moral clarity make Frolo’s evil even more apparent. Highlighting his delusion and hypocrisy.
